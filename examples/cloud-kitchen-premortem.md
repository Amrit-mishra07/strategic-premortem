## Bottom line
**Verdict: Test cheaply first.** Confidence: medium on the economics, low on demand (nothing in your message shows anyone has paid).
**What you most need to hear:**
1. "100 boxes a day by month 3" is not a forecast, it is the break-even point in a good case. On my assumed costs [A], break-even lands anywhere from ~20 to ~160 boxes/day if you sell direct, and is unreachable if margins land on the bad side or most orders go through an aggregator. That range straddles your target, so it can't be forecast from here: delivery cost and rent dominate, and measuring them is step one.
2. Your real constraint is hours, not money. Production for 100 boxes needs either a paid cook (already in my fixed costs) or roughly 25–30 founder-hours a week of hands-on work [A: ~1.5 min/box plus ~2 hrs/day logistics], which exceeds the 24 hours you three have in total and leaves nothing for selling. Either the cook is paid (cash) or the founders do it (hours), never both.
3. The cheapest, most informative move is a 4-week pre-order pilot for one or two office buildings, before you rent a kitchen.
**Do this first:** get 25 office workers to pay for a 5-day lunch subscription in advance, at ₹140.
**Flips to "Go with changes" if:** ≥20 prepaid subscribers from ≤2 buildings within 3 weeks.

*Challenge:* the hard part is reaching 100 daily buyers, not cooking. Goal-fit: if the aim is "reliable money", a pre-order subscription run from a rented shared kitchen slot is a less risky vehicle than a leased kitchen.

## Economic reality (all inputs [A])
Variable cost per box: ingredients ₹55–70, packaging ₹8–12, delivery ₹15–35, payment fees ₹3–10 → contribution ₹13–59 direct. Via an aggregator (25–35% commission) → −₹26 to +₹27. Fixed costs ₹32–53k/month (rent, one cook/helper, utilities). Break-even: 21–157 boxes/day direct; 46–75 at the favorable aggregator end; none at the bad end.
Cash: setup ₹60–90k leaves ₹2.1–2.4 lakh. With a ramp of 15 → 60 boxes/day by month 6, favorable case ends ~₹4 lakh; unfavorable case runs out around month 6 (about −₹29k).

## How it fails (top risks)
**R1 — Hours collapse.** Exams cut weekly hours from 24 to ~12 in months 3–4 [H]; the cook runs unsupervised; quality slips; repeat orders drop; cash falls; friends argue. Likelihood Plausible (30–60%). Severity Severe. Warning: founders' logged hours <18 two weeks in a row; refunds >5% of orders. Prevent: fix the roster before month 1, hire a part-time cook early, agree decision rights and what happens if someone leaves.
**R2 — Aggregator dependence.** Orders drift to the aggregator where contribution is thin or negative. Likelihood Plausible. Severity Serious. Warning: aggregator share >50% of orders. Respond: direct subscriptions with a WhatsApp ordering line, ₹10 loyalty credit for direct orders.
**R3 — Repeat rate is low.** First-week novelty, then drop-off. Warning: week-4 repeat <35% [A]. Respond: shrink menu, test a subscription.

## Decisions and thresholds
Gate 1 (week 4, pilot): ≥20 prepaid subscribers → proceed to a kitchen. <10 → change offer or segment, not location.
Gate 2 (end of month 3): contribution per box ≥₹35 and repeat ≥40% → continue; otherwise renegotiate cost or stop.
Stop-loss: ₹1.2 lakh spent without Gate 1 passed.
